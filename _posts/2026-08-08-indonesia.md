## All aboard the Walenreng

The day tour was fun, but the real reason we were there was to book a "liveaboard", which is where you live on a boat for a few days, and they take you to a bunch of good sights. I mean, it's similar to the day trip, but you get to see stuff that's not as accessible if you only have a single day (there are speedboat day trips that try to bring you to the same places, but from what I've heard they're frantically rushed).

It's common to do a 3 day, 2 night trip that starts and ends in Labuan Bajo, but we realized the 4D3N one would drop us off in Lombok (the island we wanted to go to next), letting us avoid a trip to the airport. So we signed up for that!

Here's the route:

![](/assets/images/Pasted%20image%2020260720235748.png)


It was... a hilarious circus signing up for one. Some people sign up months in advance for nice boats. As usual, we barely did any planning. There were very few that seemed to have spots for, you know, the next day, so we took what we could get... the "Walenreng".

![](/assets/images/IMG-20260704-WA0005.jpg)

It didn't look too bad in the pics, but it was a lot jankier in person. I mean, it was all *fine*, but they usually advertise these boats as kind of a luxury experience, which this one definitely wasn't. That said, we were there to see and do things, which it definitely delivered on.

Our last night was... a comical (yet definitely memorable) shitshow. They planned on getting us to the port in Lombok at ~9 PM, where we had a driver waiting for us, to get us to a hotel very late, where we'd start a hike early the next morning.

But poor planning meant we only got there around midnight. Okay, whatever. We're finally in the harbor, and... we hear a loud crunch, followed by a bunch of shouting in Indonesian, followed by about 20 minutes of engine revving. Apparently, our crew on the budget boat weren't totally familiar with "tides", because they had run us aground in the harbor.

They piled our suitcases aboard the little speedboat that took us to land, and we said goodbye to the Walenreng!

![](/assets/images/PXL_20260708_151934933.MP.jpg)

![](/assets/images/PXL_20260708_152112040.jpg)


the trip to shore was hilarious. They clearly didn't know where in the (fairly large) harbor they were, and it was night, so they were mostly shining a small flash light around, trying to find some good landing spot. There was definitely a sense of "oh we fucked up" desperation on the part of the crew, but all the passengers seemed to think it was pretty funny. The boat was surrounded by flying fish though, which was cool.

![](/assets/images/PXL_20260708_151939742.jpg)

![](/assets/images/PXL_20260708_152034510.jpg)

![](/assets/images/Pasted%20image%2020260720235716.png)

![](/assets/images/PXL_20260709_073346358.jpg)

There actually used to be a mountain named Samalas, even taller than Rinjani, but there was an absolutely [massive eruption in about 1257](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1257_Samalas_eruption), creating the caldera, which is now filled in with Lake Segara Anak.

![](/assets/images/PXL_20260709_073828751.jpg)

![](/assets/images/PXL_20260709_225429346.jpg)

the hike was awesome. I can't say if the summit would've been better, but it's hard to imagine it could be. This hike had it all: jungle! monkeys! fried bananas! dogs! a crater rim overlooking a caldera lake! amazing views!


## Gili

After the hefty hike, the plan was to relax for the rest. We took a ferry from the western side of the island to a group of three islands called "the gilis":

![](/assets/images/Pasted%20image%2020260720235655.png)

We stayed on "gili T", i.e., gili Trawangan. The main highlight on the gilis is snorkeling, but whereas often you need to be taken somewhere to snorkel, here it's RIGHT off the beach, and you're seeing incredible stuff. Most notably, large sea turtles galore. It was truly magical.
